Reasoning Models & Extended Thinking

When to spend thinking tokens and when they are pure cost — the effort control, the prompting differences, interleaved thinking inside an agent loop, and how to verify an answer whose reasoning you are not allowed to trust.

Why it matters for an FDE: every engagement now opens with "which model?", and the honest answer is a routing table with measured numbers behind it. An FDE who can show that effort=low matches effort=high on 80% of a customer's traffic just cut their inference bill without touching quality — and the 20% where it doesn't is the argument for spending more, made with evidence instead of vibes.

Skill ladder: what each score looks like

0
No exposure. Assumes "the smart model" is always better and prices it that way.
1
Awareness. Knows reasoning models exist; can name the effort levels but has never measured one.
2
Works with support. Turns effort up when output looks wrong; no numbers, no budget, no routing rule.
3
Independent production (target). Ships a routing table backed by a measured effort sweep; forecasts thinking-token cost and verifies answers independently of the trace.
4
Leads & coaches. Sets reasoning policy across an account — per-route effort, budgets, and the escalation rule — and coaches teams off "max everything".

In the field

A logistics customer routes every request to the largest model at maximum effort because "quality matters". The monthly bill arrives at $41K and procurement freezes the rollout.

The score-2 move is to switch everything to the cheap model and hope nobody notices. The score-3 FDE runs an effort sweep over 200 real requests from the logs: 83% of them score identically at effort=low, and the 17% that don't are all multi-step reconciliation tasks. One routing rule later the bill is $9K with no measured quality loss on the eval — and the reconciliation route keeps its budget, now justified in writing.

The freeze lifts in a week. What unblocked it was not a cheaper model; it was a number the CFO could check.
